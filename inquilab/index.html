01 · The theft

They learnt his face. Nobody asked.

Type his name into an AI video tool and he appears: the eyebrows, the smile, the open arms. How did these engines learn one of the most famous faces on Earth? Fans say: from his images, without a licence from him and without his consent. Fans now make him every day, in concept trailers, songs, posters and first looks for a film the studio has not even shown yet.

The tools are named by the fans themselves, in the works we have logged:

Klingnamed in a logged workSeedancenamed in a logged workGroknamed in a logged work

No consent rules. No credit for the fans who create. No share for the man whose face it is. Fans have a name for it: face theft. The fans' charge

02 · The revolt

The fans are hell-bent

The same fans who made him the King are now asking the question the industry avoids: who owns his face? They are logging the work, counting the views, comparing the tools, and organising. It looks less like a fandom and more like the start of a class action.

No lawyers yet. Just millions of fans. This page is where they sign on.

05 · The analysis

Discovery of the new oil

When oil was discovered, it did not just fuel lamps. It created cars, roads, mechanics, inventors and a whole new playground. A star's likeness is the new oil, and today it is being drilled in the dark. The fix is not to ban the drilling. It is to build the reserve properly, with the star on board. Interpretation

The wellA licensed reserve of the star: likeness, voice, looks, characters, archive. Given with consent.
→
The refineriesAI companies turn it into licensed models and tools.
→
The carsCreator tools: poster makers, trailer kits, story engines.
→
The driversFans and storytellers, everywhere.
→
The roadsYouTube, Instagram, X, Facebook.

↺ Every use pays a share back to the well. The star earns from the fans' creativity instead of fighting it, and fans create without fear.

The fans' demands: an AI law for fans

ConsentThe star decides what is in the reserve and what is not.
ShareEvery licensed use pays the star and the IP owner.
LabelsOfficial and fan-made work clearly marked. Nobody fooled.
LimitsNo deceptive deepfakes, no harmful or explicit use.
CreditFan creators named, and able to earn from their work.

INQUILAB is a fan movement and a petition, not a lawsuit, and it does not speak for Shah Rukh Khan or any studio. It calls for a fair framework, not for anyone to stop creating. The fans' charge of "face theft" is their framing of how AI tools use a star's likeness without consent; no specific company is accused of breaking the law.

The Wall II · Chennai Express, 2013: the audience moves the train

1,474 words · about 6 min read

2012: a cup, a ban and a farewell. Between the superhero and the train came a year that showed every side of the institution at once. In May 2012, after his Kolkata Knight Riders beat the Mumbai Indians at the Wankhede Stadium, he was stopped from walking onto the ground and argued with officials and security guards. The Mumbai Cricket Association banned him from the stadium for five years; he said the guards had misbehaved with children, his daughter among them. (Fact. Daily FT, 19 May 2012) Eleven days later, on 27 May 2012, the same team won its first IPL title, beating the Chennai Super Kings in the final in Chennai. (Fact. ESPNcricinfo) The ban in Mumbai was lifted in August 2015. (Fact. Deccan Chronicle, 2 Aug 2015)

On 21 October 2012 Yash Chopra died, before the release of the film he had made with him, Jab Tak Hai Jaan. It opened on 13 November 2012, grossed about ₹236 crore worldwide, and was the year's highest-grossing Hindi film overseas. (Fact. Wikipedia: Jab Tak Hai Jaan) The director who had cast him as the stalker of Darr in 1993 and the lover of Dil To Pagal Hai in 1997 closed his career with him. Interpretation

He had won a cup in Chennai and lost a stadium in Mumbai in the same month. His next film would take a train to Chennai, and this time the audience would drive. Interpretation

Every film campaign asks its audience to watch. Chennai Express asked its audience to drive.

The campaign ran for about 90 days before release in August 2013. Its central device was a train. Fans moved it along its journey with their tweets and collective activity, and as it reached each station it unlocked new content: a song, a scene, a reveal. Eyewitness account; the author was the campaign's Chief Digital Strategist A poster can only be looked at. A train you move together is a story you are part of.

The campaign's Shorty Awards entry lists a Twitter-driven train app and seven browser-based games. It also reports that the film was the first to stay in Twitter's top trends for more than 10 consecutive days, and that its opening-day Twitter activity ran more than 500% above Elysium and Planes, the Hollywood films released the same weekend. (Fact. Shorty Awards)

On launch, fans who tweeted #ChennaiExpressArrives were linked to a virtual train heading to Chennai, where the trailer was revealed early, and interactive train bogies stood at the Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore and Hyderabad airports. A mobile game, Chennai Express: Escape from Rameshwaram, built by Disney UTV's digital arm, put players in Rahul's shoes, and Best Rice and Asus integrated with the film. (Fact. exchange4media, 8 Aug 2013)

The film itself was a Rohit Shetty action comedy, produced by Red Chillies with UTV, starring Deepika Padukone opposite him. (Fact. Wikipedia: Chennai Express) The public timeline ran like a rail schedule: the first look on 1 January 2013; a 31-second title teaser sung by S. P. Balasubrahmanyam on 23 May; the trailer in Mumbai on 13 June, which passed four million YouTube views in two days; "Lungi Dance", Honey Singh's tribute to Rajinikanth, on 19 July; the Android game on 24 July; the karaoke app on 2 August. Fact. Wikipedia: Chennai Express He took it onto television too, on Comedy Nights with Kapil and Indian Idol Junior, and auctioned the costumes for charity. Fact. Wikipedia Even the satellite deal was new: Zee paid ₹48 crore plus a share tied to the box office, which the trade called the first deal of its kind in Indian cinema. Fact. Wikipedia

The system around the train:

  • an official website and social channels run as one operation
  • browser and mobile games, including "Rahul meets Meena", which used YouTube clips, ran on Facebook and took its input from Twitter (Fact. Social Samosa)
  • a virtual karaoke machine for recording and sharing the film's songs, and an interactive video in which viewers chose how the story unfolded (Fact. Gamification Nation)
  • social listening and live sentiment tracking, built with Persistent Systems: 65% positive, 31% neutral, 4% negative across social channels Fact. Gamification Nation; the campaign's own reported figures
  • more than 25 fan clubs working together on the promotion Fact. Social Samosa
  • and after release, a missed-call line: on 20 August 2013 Twitter India and ZipDial let anyone follow @iamsrk by giving a missed call, with or without a smartphone, with a stated target of reaching 50 million people in a year (Fact. Business Standard / IANS, 20 Aug 2013, which quotes the author as chief digital strategist)

What it delivered. The case study's own reported figures, not independently audited (Fact. Social Samosa, 27 Nov 2013):

It opened on 8 August 2013 with, by Persistent Systems' account, the highest opening weekend in India and the highest for a Bollywood film in the USA and Canada (Fact. PR Newswire, 18 Sept 2014). The film grossed ₹422 crore worldwide. (Fact. Sacnilk)

Box-office trackers agree on the scale if not the last crore. Wikipedia gives a worldwide range of ₹396 crore to ₹425 crore, with ₹424 crore as the final figure in one cited source; Box Office India puts the domestic net at ₹208 crore, other trade analysts at about ₹227 crore. It became the highest-grossing Bollywood film in India and worldwide at the time, ahead of 3 Idiots, the fastest to ₹100 crore net, and his biggest film in India. Fact. Wikipedia: Chennai Express; figures differ by source The train had arrived. Interpretation

The opening, in numbers. It opened on 3,550 screens in 2,550 cinemas in India and about 700 abroad: 196 in North America, 175 in Britain, 55 in the Middle East and 30 in Australia. (Fact. Wikipedia: Chennai Express; the records table gives 3,700 Indian screens) Paid previews took ₹6.75 crore, a record ahead of 3 Idiots; day one took ₹29.25 crore, second only to Ek Tha Tiger; the Indian opening weekend reached ₹93.83 crore with the previews. (Fact. Wikipedia) Abroad it opened to US$7.1 million, then a record for an Indian film: about US$2.22 million in the US and £934,118 in Britain. Fact. as above In Pakistan, where it was first pulled because four local films opened the same day, it went on to break the box-office records in Karachi. Fact. as above

The television deal. Zee bought the satellite rights for ₹48 crore, with a box-office bonus: ₹2 crore more for every ₹10 crore of net collections above ₹130 crore. It premiered on television on 20 October 2013. (Fact. Wikipedia) The rights were priced partly on the audience the campaign would build. Interpretation

What the critics said, and what the audience did. Reviews were mixed: Taran Adarsh and Filmfare's Rachit Gupta gave four stars out of five; Anupama Chopra and Rajeev Masand two; Mayank Shekhar and Raja Sen one. (Fact. Wikipedia) It received seven nominations at the 59th Filmfare Awards, including Best Film, Best Director, Best Actor and Best Actress. Fact. as above [Unverified: whether it won any Filmfare award; the Red Chillies record lists one.] The gap between the reviews and the receipts is the chapter's point: this was a film the audience had been given a part in before it opened. Interpretation

The recognition. The campaign was a finalist in the Film category of the 6th Shorty Awards (Shorty Awards), and its technology partner Persistent Systems won a Computerworld Data+ Editors' Choice award for it in September 2014. That release credits the author as the film's Creative and Digital Strategist (PR Newswire). The film won Best Marketed Film at the Screen Awards on 14 January 2014 and at the ETC Bollywood Business Awards (Fact. Wikipedia). The author's team also received the Wharton Social Media Leadership Award and an IO Awards Best Marketed Film: [Unverified]

Why it still matters. The difference between a social-media campaign and a participatory system is the difference between distributing content and giving people a role. In Chennai Express a fan's action was not a number on a dashboard. It moved the story everyone could see. That was the high point of a model the industry then walked away from for a decade.

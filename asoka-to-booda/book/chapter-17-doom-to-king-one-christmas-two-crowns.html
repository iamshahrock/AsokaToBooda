Public record Same year, two hemispheres, two careers that went from boy wonder to written off to the biggest comeback in their industries. Both once saved the world in a superhero suit, one red, one blue: Iron Man in 2008, built in a cave from a box of scraps; G.One in 2011, stepping out of a video game his creator built for his son (Chapter 9). This Christmas, at 61, both walk to the dark side of the screen: one as the villain of the Marvel universe, one as the man they call KING. Retirement plans: not found. Six rounds, no losers. The author scores it as a friendly fixture, not a fight. Interpretation; Act VIII

  • Star power. The man who built the Marvel Cinematic Universe against the last of the stars. Point to both, and to the audience.
  • Home crowd. DOOM owns the global multiplex; KING owns Indian screens and the diaspora at Christmas. Two home games in one week.
  • The character. A villain fans have waited decades to see, against a new KING nobody has seen yet. Curiosity wins either way.
  • The campaign. A studio machine with a hundred-film head start, against a fan army that makes its own posters. Both are worth studying.
  • The scheduling. Six days apart is not a clash. It is enough time to watch one, argue about it, and queue for the other.
  • The street. The real result is decided at the box office, in the reviews and over family dinners. This book will report the numbers when they exist, and not before.

The tale of the tape, played for laughs and with respect to both corners: one villain puts on a metal mask; the other does not need one, and raises an eyebrow. One snaps his fingers and half the universe goes quiet; the other opens his arms and the other half comes running. One's fans stay for the post-credits scene; the other's stand outside his gate in Mumbai every 2 November. Interpretation; Act VIII

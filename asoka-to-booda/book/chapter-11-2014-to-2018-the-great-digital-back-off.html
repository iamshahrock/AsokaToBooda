Part Three · Asoka to Boo-Da · Mute Button, 2014 to 2022

In 2001 he played Asoka, the emperor who conquered everything, saw the cost at Kalinga, and gave up his sword for the Buddha's path. This thesis bends that story. Between Asoka and the Buddha sits the "boo": the jeers after Zero, the trolling, the October the whole country watched. Boo-Da is the man on the other side of the boos, someone the noise no longer reaches. Interpretation

This Part follows that passage in order: the back-off from 2014, the fall of Zero in 2018, and the four years of silence that ended with an empty road outside Mannat in October 2021. (Interpretation)

Chapter 11

2014 to 2018: the great digital back-off

1,242 words · about 5 min read

The train reached Chennai in August 2013. Then, film by film, the man who had driven it began to step off. Interpretation

The films kept coming. The star kept smiling. But something changed in the way they reached people. The man who had led every campaign from the front began, film by film, to step back from it. (Interpretation; from the author's essay Asoka to Boo-Da*, Act I)*

2014: a tour and a trophy. The first film after the train was Farah Khan's Happy New Year, a heist comedy, and its campaign went back to the stage. Before release the cast toured North America and Britain as SLAM! The Tour, opening at Houston's Toyota Center in September 2014, his first visit to the city in a decade. (Fact. ABC13 Houston, Sept 2014) It grossed ₹397 crore worldwide. Fact. Sacnilk On the cricket field his Kolkata Knight Riders won their second IPL title on 1 June 2014. Fact. Wikipedia: Kolkata Knight Riders The money and the trophies held up. Interpretation

2015: fifty, and a sentence. On 2 November 2015, his fiftieth birthday, he told an interviewer there was "extreme intolerance" in the country. Politicians attacked him, and his next film, Dilwale, met protests in parts of India; in Mangaluru, Bajrang Dal activists stopped screenings. (Fact. The News Minute, 1 Mar 2016; The News Minute) Dilwale, his reunion with Kajol, still grossed about ₹408 crore worldwide. Fact. Filmibeat, 2023

Four months later, launching the trailer of Fan, he said he had never called India intolerant and had been misunderstood. Asked about the fallout, he made a joke about cricket and then said: "Freedom of speech means the right to keep silent also." Fact. The News Minute, 1 Mar 2016 The showman who had answered every question for twenty-five years had found a new position: silence as a right. Interpretation

2016: a film about a fan. Fan, released on 15 April 2016, was about the thing he knew best: an obsessive young man from Delhi, Gaurav, who looks exactly like his idol, a film star named Aryan Khanna, and turns on him when the star rejects him. He played both. (Fact. Wikipedia: Fan) The campaign was built around real fans. The teaser came out on his birthday, 2 November 2015. The anthem "Jabra Fan" was released on 1 February 2016 and recorded in several languages, including Arabic. The logo was made from photographs of real fans, and graffiti was painted on his house. The trailer drew four million views in 24 hours. (Fact. Wikipedia: Fan; Gulf News) Made for about ₹85 crore, it grossed about ₹182 crore to ₹188 crore worldwide, below expectations for a star of his scale. Fact. Wikipedia: Fan; figures differ by source

How he became his own fan. Fan was directed by Maneesh Sharma for Yash Raj Films. The prosthetic make-up that turned him into the young Gaurav was designed by the Hollywood make-up artist Greg Cannom; he was 3D-scanned for the younger face, changed his voice for the part, and Red Chillies VFX did the effects. It is the most revealing film of the decade for this book. A star made a film about the danger of a fan who believes he owns the star, in the same years he was pulling back from his fans. Interpretation Later that year Dear Zindagi, in which he played a supporting role as a therapist, became the first Hindi film to market itself on Tinder. (Fact. exchange4media, 14 Nov 2016)

2017: the train again, and a death. For Raees he went back to the idea that had worked in 2013, a train, but in person. On 23 and 24 January 2017 he rode the August Kranti Rajdhani from Mumbai to Delhi, stopping at Vadodara, Ratlam, Kota and Mathura to meet fans on the platforms. At Vadodara the crowd pushed against the windows; police used lathis, one man died and two were injured. He called it "really unfortunate". (Fact. Business Standard / IANS, 24 Jan 2017; The Tribune) Raees grossed about ₹272 crore worldwide. Fact. Filmibeat, 2023

In 2013 the train had been virtual, and the fans had moved it with their thumbs. In 2017 it was real, and a fan died on the platform. The direct line had become dangerous. Read the column on the right and a pattern appears. The money held up: Happy New Year and Dilwale each crossed ₹390 crore, and even the films called disappointments earned more than most stars' hits. What thinned out was the story around the films. Each campaign was a clever stunt, but no longer a movement led by the man himself. Interpretation; the author's essay, Act II

Every campaign had a clever idea: a tour, a dating app, a train, an AR lens. None was a system. Chennai Express gave the audience a role that carried across every channel for 90 days. Its successors gave the audience a moment, then moved on. Interpretation The innovation never stopped; Zero alone would launch an AR first. What stopped was the founder leading the story himself, and the architecture that turned fans into participants. Interpretation

Why did the man who wrote the playbook stop reading from it? Was it fatigue? Was it a strategy? Or was it something nobody outside the room could see? Who was running the campaigns now, and did they know what they were holding? The author's questions, Act I The record shows three things that happened in these years: a sentence on his fiftieth birthday that turned a film into a target, a film about a fan who turned on his star, and a real train where a fan died. This book does not claim to know which of them mattered most. It leaves the questions where they belong: in the reader's hands. Interpretation

Then came Zero.

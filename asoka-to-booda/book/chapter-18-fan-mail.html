A new oil, and nobody holds the licence. When oil was found, the car followed, then the road, the mechanic, the driver and the whole economy around them. A star's face is the oil of the AI era. Today fans drill it without a licence, AI companies refine it without paying for it, and the people who own it have not yet built a pipeline. The opportunity is an official reserve: the star's likeness, voice and worlds, opened to storytellers on fair terms. The star earns from the fans' creativity instead of fighting it, and fans create without fear. Interpretation; Act IX

KTVU / AP, Jan 2024) Those were defences. What the KING fans began in 2026 was a demand. Interpretation

The fan exception. The Jackie Shroff order drew a line fans can use. It barred commercial use of his name, voice and image, but declined to act against a fan's "Thug Life" compilation video, which it read as a compliment and creative expression. (Fact. S.S. Rana) Tribute was protected; trade was not. That is the line INQUILAAB asks a law to write down. Interpretation

The fans have a word for how AI tools use a famous face without consent: face theft. Their answer is INQUILAAB, a movement under #FreeMyFace that asks for an AI law for fans in five words: consent, share, labels, limits, credit. It is not a lawsuit, and it does not speak for the star or any studio. Interpretation

INQUILAAB has a chant instead of a lawyer: "Inquilaab King ke saath, aao aao leke apna haath." Fact: the INQUILAAB page at agarmainkinghota.com; the author's essay, Act IX Its five demands spell out the AI law the fans want: consent, the star decides what is in the reserve; share, every licensed use pays the star and the owner of the work; labels, official and fan-made work clearly marked; limits, no deceptive deepfakes and no harmful or explicit use; credit, fan creators named and able to earn. Fact: the INQUILAAB manifesto

The campaign applies the rule to itself. Its poster studio sends the film poster to the AI blurred, so no star's face reaches the model, tells the model to depict no real actor or public figure, and labels every finished poster "AI-made fan poster". Fact: the Make Your Poster studio at agarmainkinghota.com, 12 Oct 2026 Its slogan is three words: "Your face. Not his." Fact

Ahead of the rule. India's February 2026 rules ask two things of realistic AI media: a prominent label and permanent provenance metadata. (Fact. Hogan Lovells) The fan studio does both: every finished poster carries the visible label and an AI provenance note written into the image file itself. Fact: the Make Your Poster studio at agarmainkinghota.com, 12 Oct 2026 The rules are written for platforms, not fan sites; the studio follows them anyway. Interpretation

A working model: Lairs Cinema. The campaign's story game, King's Lairs, publishes its set designs: a glass dome on a frozen lake, a casino carved under a prison, a dance pit, an aquarium den. Fans animate them on their own AI accounts, star in them with their own faces, post the result and are credited and rewarded for it. The world is licensed and no face is stolen. If it works for a fan site, it can work for a studio. Interpretation; Act IX

The circle closes here. In 2011 the author's team built a hero who lived online before he lived on screen. In 2026 the audience builds the hero itself. The question is no longer whether fans will make the work. It is who owns the face when they do. Interpretation

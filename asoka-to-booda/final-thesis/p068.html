PART IX — THE INDUSTRY PROPOSITION

15. From marketing a film to building a relationship

The historical case offers a useful way to distinguish three models of entertainment marketing.

Model Primary asset Primary activity Potential measure
Campaign-led marketing The upcoming film Advertising, publicity, trailers and release promotion Reach, engagement, ticket sales
Ecosystem-led marketing The film and its associated intellectual property Games, merchandise, characters, digital extensions and partnerships Participation, licensing, ancillary revenue
Participatory entertainment The film, its intellectual property and the audience's creative activity Fan creation, interactive experiences, community events and licensed participation Repeat participation, original contributions, creator value and commercial conversion

The first two models have historical examples in the Ra.One and Chennai Express campaigns.

The third is a proposed framework.

The difference is not simply that the third model uses AI.

It changes the role of the audience.

In a conventional campaign, the audience receives and responds to promotional material.

In a participatory model, the audience may contribute original work, collaborate with others, play, compete, organise or create within an agreed framework.

That requires technology, but also governance, rights, consent and trust.

Five propositions for future research

Proposition 1 — Digital campaigns can create assets beyond attention.

The Ra.One record demonstrates the use of character guides, merchandise, games and publishing. Further research could establish which assets retained value after the theatrical campaign ended.

Proposition 2 — Analytics and creative participation are different capabilities.

Chennai Express provides evidence of large-scale social-media measurement. A future system would need to measure not only attention but also original participation and repeat engagement.

Proposition 3 — A quieter public campaign does not necessarily imply less technology.

The 2014–2018 period requires production-side and campaign-level evidence before a change in digital strategy can be established.

Proposition 4 — Fan communities may provide continuity between releases.

Their scale, autonomy, creative output and economic relationships with rights holders require systematic study rather than assumptions about a single, coordinated fan network.

Proposition 5 — AI could lower the cost of participation, but rights determine sustainability.

Interactive media could expand the range of creative experiences available to audiences. The model's viability depends on consent, attribution, licensing, privacy and fair treatment of creators.

These propositions make the case study useful to marketers, media companies, researchers, technology businesses and intellectual-property owners.

They also create a research agenda that can be tested beyond the Shah Rukh Khan case.

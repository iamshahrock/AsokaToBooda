Chapter 36 · Who Owns the Face: Inquilaab

Before the studio had shown more than a teaser, fans began making their own KING with AI: concept trailers, first looks, songs and scenes, several naming the tools that made them, Kling, Seedance, Grok. The campaign’s daily scan had found and linked 40 such works by 18 October 2026; the 21 that show public view counts had 2,883,120 views between them. (Fact: the campaign’s AI creator log, 18 Oct 2026. A floor, not a census.)

The Fan-made AI Universe. (The campaign's art, agarmainkinghota.com)

A new oil, and nobody holds the licence. When oil was found, the car followed, then the road, the mechanic, the driver and a whole economy. A star’s face is the oil of the AI era. Fans drill it without a licence, AI companies refine it without paying for it, and the people who own it have not yet built a pipeline. The opportunity is an official reserve: the star’s likeness, voice and worlds opened to storytellers on fair terms, so the star earns from the fans’ creativity instead of fighting it, and fans create without fear. (Analysis)

The rulebook, 2022 to 2026. In four years Indian courts and an Indian ministry wrote the first working rules for a famous face. The Delhi High Court protected Amitabh Bachchan’s name, voice and image on 25 November 2022, Anil Kapoor’s on 20 September 2023, including AI morphing, Jackie Shroff’s on 15 May 2024, and Aishwarya Rai Bachchan’s on 9 September 2025; Abhishek Bachchan, Akshay Kumar, Ajay Devgn, R. Madhavan and Kajol followed into early 2026. From 20 February 2026 India’s amended IT Rules require realistic AI media to be labelled, carry permanent metadata, and come down within three hours of a lawful order. (Fact. The Week, 25 Nov 2022; Business Today, 20 Sep 2023; Mondaq, 27 May 2024; The Hollywood Reporter India, 16 Sep 2025; Storyboard18; Hogan Lovells) Abroad, Tennessee’s ELVIS Act (2024) added voice to likeness rights, and the US TAKE IT DOWN Act (2025) set a 48-hour removal rule for non-consensual intimate images. (Fact. AP via News4JAX, 21 Mar 2024; Akin Gump)

The name not on the list. Three of his KING co-stars, Abhishek Bachchan, Anil Kapoor and Jackie Shroff, hold court orders over their own likeness. This book found no public record that Shah Rukh Khan has sought one. (Fact, as sourced above) MediaNama’s tracker of personality-rights cases in the Delhi High Court, published in September 2026, lists more than forty people and companies, among them Salman Khan (July 2026) and Manoj Bajpayee (September 2026), his old Theatre Action Group colleague. Shah Rukh Khan is not on it. (Fact. MediaNama, Sept 2026) [Unverified] the Bombay High Court’s record. On the public record, much of KING’s cast is protected from AI copies by a court; its lead is protected mainly by his fans. (Analysis)

The fan exception. The Jackie Shroff order barred commercial use of his name, voice and image, but declined to act against a fan’s “Thug Life” compilation video. (Fact. S.S. Rana) Tribute was protected; trade was not. That is the line Inquilaab asks a law to write down. (Analysis)

INQUILAAB. The fans have a word for how AI tools use a famous face without consent: face theft. Their answer is INQUILAAB, spelt in a nod to Amitabh Bachchan’s film Inquilaab, under the hashtag #FreeMyFace. Fans have defended stars before: in January 2024 Taylor Swift’s fans flooded X to bury explicit AI fakes of her. (Fact. KTVU / AP, Jan 2024) Those were defences. Inquilaab is a demand, and its five words spell the AI law the fans want: consent, the star decides what goes in the reserve; share, every licensed use pays the star and the owner of the work; labels, official and fan-made work clearly marked; limits, no deceptive deepfakes and no harmful or explicit use; credit, fan creators named and able to earn. It is not a lawsuit, and it does not speak for the star or any studio. (Fact: the Inquilaab manifesto at agarmainkinghota.com; Proposal)

The campaign applies the rule to itself. Its poster studio sends the film poster to the AI blurred, so no star’s face reaches the model; tells the model to depict no real actor or public figure; and marks every finished poster “AI-made fan poster”, with an AI provenance note written into the image file. Its slogan is four words: “Your face. Not his.” (Fact: the Make Your Poster studio at agarmainkinghota.com, 12 Oct 2026) India’s 2026 rules ask platforms for exactly those two things, a label and provenance. The rules are written for platforms, not fan sites; the studio follows them anyway. (Analysis)

The circle closes here. In 2000 his website put the fans’ faces around his. In 2011 the author’s team built a hero who lived online before he lived on screen. In 2021 a chocolate brand licensed his face to small shopkeepers, with his consent. In 2026 the audience builds the hero itself. The question is no longer whether fans will make the work. It is who owns the face when they do. Will the first AI law for fans be written in a courtroom, or in a comment section? (Analysis; the author’s questions)

Chapter 33 · The 2000 Playbook, Revisited

Everything in this Part is labelled Proposal or Analysis unless it says otherwise. SRKWorld’s return, ijOO, the AI Bioskope and the fan-franchising operating system are the author’s concepts. None is an announced plan of Shah Rukh Khan, Red Chillies Entertainment, the makers of KING or any company. Rumour has it something is cooking. This Part is about what could be cooked.

Take a leaf out of his own book. In 2000 he tried to build a direct, permanent line to his audience, with his own name on the address, and it failed because India had no broadband, no smartphones and no habit of looking at a small screen for a star. (Fact; Analysis, Part II) In 2026 all three exist in the hands of hundreds of millions of people, and so does the technology to let them make things, not just watch them. (Analysis)

On TRAI’s own counts, mobile subscriptions grew about 680-fold between March 2000 and July 2026. (Analysis, arithmetic on the table)

The makers are already there. The third thing missing in 2000 was a habit, and the habit now pays. Boston Consulting Group counted 2 to 2.5 million monetised content creators in India in May 2025, and estimated that creators influence roughly US$350 to 400 billion of consumer spending, a figure it projects past US$1 trillion by 2030. (Fact. BCG, From Content to Commerce, 3 May 2025. BCG’s count covers creators who earn from their content, not everyone who posts.)

So the 2000 playbook no longer fails on infrastructure. In 2000 a website had to bring a scattered audience to one address; in 2026 the audience carries the address in its pocket, and millions of its members already make things for a living. What the S.T.E.A.M. table still lacks is not a pipe or a habit but a system that lets fans run all five disciplines around one film, all year. (Analysis)

EY India, 24 Mar 2026) In 2000 a fan paid nothing to reach a star online because there was nothing to pay for; in 2025 more than a hundred million Indian homes paid every month to watch. (Analysis)

Merchandising is still the weak discipline. Worldwide, licensed merchandise and services sold US$389.8 billion at retail in 2025, up 5.45%, with character and entertainment licensing the largest category. (Fact. License Global, 27 Aug 2026) In India, Storyboard18 reported, entertainment and character licensing makes up 26% of the business, against 41% worldwide, and a brand consultant set the top IPL teams’ US$2 to 3 million a year from merchandise against Manchester United’s more than US$100 million. (Fact: what the article reports. Storyboard18, 11 Dec 2024) Of the five disciplines, the one his campaigns tried least often since 2011 is also the one India has built least. (Analysis)

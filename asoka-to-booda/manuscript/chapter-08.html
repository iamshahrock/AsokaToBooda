Chapter 8 · The KKR Years: the institution, 2004 to 2009

[image: SRKWorld.com, 2000: Opening Office]

The Kolkata Knight Riders squad, the team he bought at the first IPL auction in 2008. (Image: Final Thesis archive)

SRKWorld.com, 2000: “Opening Office”, with Dreamz Unlimited, Arc Lights, Dreamz TV and The Vision. (Archive: the author’s collection)

He came out of 2003 with a repaired spine and without his first company. What he did next was to stop being only a star and start building institutions around himself. (Analysis)

A studio of his own. Red Chillies Entertainment, which he and Gauri Khan registered in 2002, absorbed what remained of Dreamz Unlimited, with Gauri Khan as producer. (Fact. Wikipedia: Red Chillies Entertainment) [Unverified] the dates of incorporation and of the merger; sources give 2002, 2003 and 2004, and the Registrar of Companies would settle them. Its first release, Farah Khan’s Main Hoon Na (2004), was a hit. The year’s biggest film was also his, Yash Chopra’s Veer-Zaara, and the third, Swades, in which he played a NASA scientist who comes home to a village without electricity, won him the Filmfare Award for Best Actor. (Fact) By 2004 he was no longer playing one kind of hero. He was playing the country’s idea of itself. (Analysis)

The tools before the hero. In 2006 Red Chillies quietly set up its own visual-effects house, Red Chillies VFX. It would work on Chak De! India and Don, and later on the effects of Ra.One. (Fact. Wikipedia: Red Chillies Entertainment) The company that would one day build a superhero started by building the tools. (Analysis)

The honours and the defeats. The Padma Shri came in 2005. (Fact) In January 2007 he replaced Amitabh Bachchan as the host of Kaun Banega Crorepati for one season of 53 episodes; it averaged about 6.8 TVR against about 14.1 and 11.1 for Bachchan’s first two seasons. (Fact. Wikipedia: Kaun Banega Crorepati) It was a rare public defeat, taken on prime-time television. (Analysis) In April 2007 Madame Tussauds in London unveiled his wax figure; in June France made him an Officier of the Ordre des Arts et des Lettres. (Fact. The Tribune, 4 Apr 2007; Filmibeat, 21 June 2007)

2007: both halves. In Chak De! India he played a disgraced Muslim hockey player who wins back his country by making others win; it brought another Filmfare Best Actor award. (Fact; Analysis) Then on 9 November 2007 came Red Chillies’ own Om Shanti Om: about 2,000 prints worldwide, then the widest release for an Indian film, a tie-in with Amul, and a worldwide gross of about ₹150 crore to ₹152 crore on a ₹40 crore budget. It introduced Deepika Padukone. (Fact. Wikipedia: Om Shanti Om; the two gross figures differ slightly) It opened on the same Friday as Saawariya, the first Hindi film backed by a Hollywood studio, Sony Pictures, and beat it at home. (Fact. Celluloid Junkie, 11 Nov 2007) The star, the producer and the owner of the effects studio were now one person. (Analysis)

20 February 2008: the team. At the first Indian Premier League auction he bought the Kolkata franchise with Juhi Chawla and her husband Jay Mehta for US$75.09 million and named it Kolkata Knight Riders. (Fact. Wikipedia: Kolkata Knight Riders) The partners were familiar: Juhi Chawla from Dreamz Unlimited, Jay Mehta from the SRKWorld launch table. (Fact) On the league’s first night, 18 April 2008, Brendon McCullum made 158 not out off 73 balls and the Knight Riders won by 140 runs. The team finished sixth of eight, and still reported a first-season profit of ₹13 crore. (Fact. Wisden; Wikipedia: 2008 KKR season) A team that lost more than it won made money; the asset was the audience, not the table. (Analysis)

The line someone else opened. On 17 April 2008, the day before the league began, Amitabh Bachchan started a blog and kept it going for more than ten years. (Fact. Gulf News, 5 Nov 2018) The man he had replaced on Kaun Banega Crorepati now had the kind of direct line SRKWorld had been. (Analysis)

2009: the line goes wrong. In the second season, played in South Africa, an anonymous blog called Fake IPL Player posted invented inside stories about his team: 43 posts in four weeks, about 17,000 comments, about 150,000 visitors on one day. The team finished last, with three wins in fourteen matches, and lost all nine games decided in the final over. (Fact. Wisden; ESPNcricinfo) For the first time the internet he had tried to build on in 2000 was talking about him without him. (Analysis) A film can be recut before release. A team loses in public, every night, for six weeks.

In December 2008 Newsweek named him one of the fifty most powerful people in the world and “the world’s biggest movie star”. (Fact. Filmibeat, 23 Dec 2008) He was a star, a studio owner, a team owner, a television host, the face of the country’s second-largest carmaker and a holder of the Padma Shri. He had built everything except the thing he had tried first: his own line to the audience, owned by him and open every day. (Analysis)

Then, on 14 August 2009, immigration officers at Newark airport in New Jersey took him aside and questioned him for over an hour, he said two, because of his name. (Fact. Wikipedia: My Name Is Khan, citing the BBC) His next film was called My Name Is Khan.

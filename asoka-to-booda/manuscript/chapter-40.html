The board comes home. Chess began as an Indian army game. Chaturanga, Sanskrit for “having four parts”, named the four divisions of an army: elephants, chariots, cavalry and infantry. It was played on an uncheckered eight-by-eight board called the ashtāpada, and the earliest clear reference to it comes from the Gupta Empire in the sixth century AD. (Fact. Wikipedia: Chaturanga, consulted 26 Oct 2026) Its foot soldier, the padāti, is the ancestor of the pawn that this chapter turns into a lighter. (Fact. Wikipedia: Chaturanga)

Eighty-one squares have been played before. Japan’s shogi uses a board of nine ranks by nine files, 81 squares, with 20 pieces a side, 40 in all, nine of them pawns. Its signature rule lets a player keep captured pieces “in hand” and drop them back into play as their own. (Fact. Wikipedia: Shogi, consulted 26 Oct 2026) The earliest accepted written mention dates from 1058 to 1064, and the present form was played by the 15th century. (Fact. Wikipedia: Shogi) So Chess81 is not the first game on a nine-by-nine board; its claim is its own 36-piece rule set and its story. (Analysis) A captured piece changing sides is the same idea as a fan crossing from the audience onto the board. (Analysis)

The market the board walks into. On 12 December 2024 in Singapore, Gukesh Dommaraju, aged 18, beat China’s Ding Liren 7.5 to 6.5 over a 14-game match for a $2.5 million prize fund. He became the youngest world champion, a record Garry Kasparov had held since 1985. (Fact. Al Jazeera, 12 Dec 2024) Online, Chess.com passed 200 million registered members in April 2025, with more than 10 million games a day and 85% of new sign-ups from outside the United States. (Fact. Techleap Finder, May 2025) India ranks second on the site by membership; the United States and India together hold 84 million members. (Fact. Chess.com, updated 12 Aug 2026) India’s own split of that 84 million: N/A in the source. (Method note)

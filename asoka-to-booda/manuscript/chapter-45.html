Chapter 45 · MY SUM: the Royal Flush Challenge

(Proposal) The feature that makes O TO X more than a quiz is MY SUM: two or more players combine what they made. One player’s holiday tile, a second player’s 100-word innovation, a third player’s rival or plot twist become one new universe, a playable mission and a new game asset, credited to everyone who put a card in. Each contributor keeps a defined share of the points.

KING EXPRESS: the train the fans drive. (Image: Final Thesis archive)

That is the Royal Flush, played by readers. Five cards, from five hands, make one hand nobody could have dealt alone. (Analysis)

The cycle it is built to start: players create assets, assets combine, combinations become new games, new games bring new players, and new players create more assets. The game grows through the players’ imagination instead of depending on a single star or a single film. (Proposal)

From 100 words to a card. A player pastes an idea of exactly 100 words, and the game can help turn it into a character profile, an illustrated card, a short story, a mission or a challenge. The player chooses what to accept, edits the result, and owns only what they actually have the rights to use. (Proposal)

The 60-second World Championship. Daily competitions, themed rounds, team battles, seasons and regional leaderboards, so that a player in Mumbai can meet a player in New York under the same clock and the same rules. The score of a combined hand never pretends to be a financial valuation: points are points. (Proposal)

The rules that protect the player. Personal photographs are private by default. Every player keeps a vault of their own ideas, with timestamps and credits, and decides whether each is private, shared with friends or licensed for use in the game. Real celebrities’ names, likenesses and film artwork need the owners’ permission for any commercial use, so the game starts with original and player-owned material only. Moderation, originality checks and reporting keep it fair as it grows. (Proposal; the same rules as Inquilaab, Part VII)

Why a Royal Flush is the right name. A standard 52-card deck can be dealt into 2,598,960 different five-card hands, and only 4 of them are royal flushes, one per suit: a chance of 1 in 649,740 on a single deal. (Fact. Neil E. Cotter, University of Utah, Probability: Combinatorics example 15) A hand that rare almost never arrives by luck from one dealer; MY SUM turns it into something players build on purpose, one card from each hand. (Analysis)

Why points stay points. India’s Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 received the President’s assent on 22 August 2025. It bans online money games, defined as games played with monetary stakes or deposits in the hope of financial reward, and allows e-sports and social games played without stakes. (Fact. SCC Online Blog, 24 Aug 2025; Business Today, 22 Aug 2025) Offering a money game can bring up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to ₹1 crore; advertising one, up to 2 years or ₹50 lakh. (Fact. SCC Online Blog, 24 Aug 2025) Within days Dream11 stopped its paid contests, MPL disabled deposits for real-money games, Zupee said it would drop its paid titles and PokerBaazi paused all operations. (Fact. Business Today, 22 Aug 2025) A game built for Indian players in 2026 therefore keeps its scores as game points with no cash value, which is the rule this chapter already set: the score of a combined hand is never a valuation. (Analysis)

What player-made worlds can earn elsewhere. The largest working example of players building the game is Roblox. Its annual report for 2024 says developers and creators earned $922.8 million worth of Robux that year, up from $740.8 million in 2023, and that over 17,000 of them exchanged their earned Robux for money in 2024. (Fact. Roblox Corporation, Form 10-K for fiscal 2024, SEC filing) MY SUM borrows the shape of that loop, players creating assets that bring in more players, but not the cash-out: under the 2025 Act an Indian version would keep shares of credit and points, not money. (Analysis)

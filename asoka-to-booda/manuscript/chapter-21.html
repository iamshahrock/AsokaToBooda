Chapter 21 · Prison Nights

Of the four weeks between the raid and the homecoming, the public knows almost nothing. (Analysis)

What is documented fits in a few lines: the courts that refused bail and the court that granted it; a father’s twenty-minute conversation with his son over a prison intercom on 21 October; officers at his gate the same day for paperwork; a son walking out of Arthur Road on 30 October to a crowd that had, by then, found its way back to Mannat. (Fact, as sourced in Chapter 20)

What is not documented is everything else: what was said, what was felt, what a family did with those nights. The man whose every word had been quoted for thirty years did not describe them, and this book does not imagine them. The private turmoil stays private. (Method note)

One thing can be said without imagining anything. The two most public men in this story, the father and the son, spent those weeks being looked at by a country, and neither of them spoke. (Analysis)

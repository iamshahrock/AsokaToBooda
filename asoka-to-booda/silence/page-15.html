2014–2018: A COMPARISON OF THE UNDERLYING MARKETING MODELS

From integrated digital ecosystems to campaign-specific experimentation

15.1 The evolution was not from innovation to no innovation

The strongest conclusion supported by the campaign record is not that digital innovation disappeared after Chennai Express.

Happy New Year introduced gaming and personalised promotional content. Fan explored the relationship between a superstar and an obsessive admirer. Dear Zindagi used a Tinder India collaboration to connect the film's themes with a digital platform. Jab Harry Met Sejal experimented with serial promotional content. ZERO incorporated character-led digital experiences, voice technology and augmented reality.

These are meaningful examples of creative and technological experimentation.

The more precise distinction is that the earlier RA.ONE and Chennai Express campaigns sought to connect several elements into a wider system, while many later innovations were more closely associated with individual films, platforms or release campaigns.

The apparent gap is in sustained ecosystem-building—not in the existence of digital marketing.

15.2 The three phases of the period

PHASE I · 2011–2013 Build the experience

RA.ONE · Don 2 · Chennai Express

Digital technology could become an entertainment product or participation mechanism in its own right: a game, character extension, interactive experience or collective audience activity.

The Chennai Express case study describes technology as an enabler of creative strategy, audience insight and real-time adaptation.

PHASE II · 2014–2016 Campaign extensions

Happy New Year · Dilwale · Fan · Dear Zindagi

Digital innovation continued through gaming, personalised content, character storytelling and brand-platform collaborations.

The available campaign record, however, more often describes film-specific activations than a single, enduring system connecting games, fan participation, intellectual property and continuing digital products.

PHASE III · 2017–2018 Character-led platform innovation

Jab Harry Met Sejal · ZERO

The campaigns explored platform capabilities including short-form serial content, camera effects, messaging stickers, voice interfaces, augmented reality and rewards.

ZERO is particularly important because it demonstrates that substantial digital experimentation continued within the period.

15.3 The underlying comparison

The distinction becomes clearer when the campaigns are compared across the same dimensions.

Dimension RA.ONE / Chennai Express benchmark Common pattern in later campaigns
Role of technology Creates interactive experiences, digital products and campaign participation mechanisms. Distributes, personalises or amplifies film-specific promotional content, with notable exceptions.
Audience role Player, contributor, participant and, in some mechanics, a driver of campaign progress. Viewer, sharer, follower, contest entrant or participant in a specific platform activation.
Product lifespan Games, character extensions and digital experiences offer potential continuity beyond the immediate campaign. Many activations are tied to the film's promotional window or a particular platform.
Measurement The Chennai Express case study describes campaign analytics and daily creative recalibration. Public documentation more commonly describes engagement, sharing and platform features than a comparable end-to-end feedback architecture.
Commercial extension Character licensing, games, merchandise, brands and film promotion form connected elements of the franchise ambition. Brand collaborations, promotional partnerships and rewards are more commonly documented as film-specific activities.

This table compares documented campaign models, not every marketing activity undertaken by the films. “Common pattern” describes the material reviewed and should not be interpreted as proof that no other activity occurred.

15.4 The platform trap

The maturation of digital platforms presents a plausible explanation for the changing form of film marketing.

As social media and mobile applications became more widely used, campaigns could reach audiences through established third-party platforms without necessarily developing bespoke websites, games or permanent digital infrastructure.

This offered potential advantages in speed, distribution and access to existing audiences.

It also raised a strategic question: when a campaign relies increasingly on third-party platforms, does it build a lasting relationship with the audience, or does it rent access to that audience for the duration of a release?

The platform trap is a hypothesis for this investigation, not an established explanation of Shah Rukh Khan's decisions. Testing it would require comparative evidence about campaign investment, audience retention, ownership of digital assets and the continuation of fan relationships between releases.

15.5 The franchise gap

A successful campaign does not automatically create a sustainable franchise.

A digital game, character-led experience or merchandise line requires continuing creative development, rights management, distribution, technical maintenance and commercial support.

A campaign may generate considerable attention without producing an asset that remains viable after the film's release.

The RA.ONE and Chennai Express benchmarks make this distinction particularly relevant. They show the ambition to connect film, technology and audience activity. The later campaigns raise the question of whether those capabilities were maintained as an ongoing institutional model.

The evidence establishes a change in the publicly visible campaign architecture. It does not yet establish why that change occurred, or whether it reflected a deliberate strategic decision.

The Central Question: Will KING Redefine Bollywood Marketing Once Again?

The research question and the industry proposition

3.1 From a film campaign to a marketing model

The central question of this thesis is deliberately ambitious:

Will KING redefine Bollywood marketing once again—and design a new marketing path for the market to learn and follow?

The question is not a prediction of the film's commercial performance. Nor does it assume that a new marketing model has already been approved, implemented or endorsed by Shah Rukh Khan or Red Chillies Entertainment.

It is an investigation into an opportunity.

The fifteen-year chronology raises the possibility that the next phase of film marketing could draw on several developments simultaneously:

The ability to build recognisable characters and storyworlds that extend beyond a single theatrical release.

The capacity of digital platforms to connect audiences, creators, fan communities and commercial partners.

The growing possibilities of participatory storytelling, including fan-generated content, games, challenges, memes and interactive experiences.

The need for clear intellectual-property rights, transparent contest rules, consent, moderation and responsible use of celebrity likenesses.

The opportunity to measure not merely exposure, but meaningful participation, creator involvement and the quality of audience engagement.

Taken together, these developments provide a basis for asking whether a film campaign can evolve into a more structured and sustained creative ecosystem.

3.2 The hypothesis

The working hypothesis is that the next evolution of Bollywood marketing may lie in combining the reach of a major cinematic release with a governed, participatory digital environment.

In such a model, the film remains the creative centre. The surrounding campaign creates opportunities for audiences to engage with its themes, characters and fictional world through authorised activities and clearly defined forms of participation.

The audience is no longer considered only as a recipient of promotional messages. Subject to the rights and permissions involved, it may also participate as a creator, storyteller, community organiser or contributor to a wider cultural conversation.

This hypothesis will be examined against the historical case studies, rather than accepted as a conclusion in advance.

3.3 The proposed research questions

RESEARCH QUESTION 1

Historical innovation

What did RA.ONE and Chennai Express attempt that distinguished their digital campaigns, and what can be established about their execution and documented outcomes?

RESEARCH QUESTION 2

Brand and audience evolution

How did the relationship between Shah Rukh Khan's personal brand, film characters, fan communities, technology and commercial partners change between 2011 and 2026?

RESEARCH QUESTION 3

The KING opportunity

Which campaign mechanisms and creative concepts from the historical investigation could be adapted into a future-facing, rights-cleared marketing framework for KING?

RESEARCH QUESTION 4

Industry application

What would studios, brand partners, technology platforms and fan communities need to establish before such a framework could be tested, evaluated and potentially adopted?

3.4 Scope, method and evidentiary standard

This is a cinematic industry case study combining historical campaign analysis, documented campaign materials, the author's professional contribution and personal recollections, and forward-looking creative proposals.

Its evidence will be separated into four categories:

Evidence category Treatment in the thesis
Publicly documented campaign activity Supported by identifiable campaign materials, official sources or contemporaneous reporting.
Campaign performance figures Attributed to the original case study or named source, with methodology and limitations stated wherever available.
Author's professional experience and recollections Presented in the first person where appropriate and distinguished from independently established facts.
Future concepts and recommendations Identified as proposals, with implementation, rights and commercial feasibility subject to validation.

This distinction is central to the credibility of the investigation. It allows the thesis to retain its creative ambition without confusing historical evidence, personal interpretation and proposed future activity.

The proposition

The investigation begins with RA.ONE, moves through fifteen years of campaign evolution, and arrives at KING—not with a predetermined verdict, but with a question for the industry.

Can the next great Bollywood marketing innovation be more than a campaign for a film? Can it become a platform through which cinema, technology, brands and audiences participate in a shared creative universe?

That is the question this thesis sets out to investigate.

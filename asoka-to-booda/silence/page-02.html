Fifteen Years of Waves: From RA.ONE to KING

2011–2026 | The evolution of a cinematic and digital ecosystem

Fifteen years provide a useful window through which to examine the evolution of Shah Rukh Khan's relationship with digital marketing, brand building and the changing business of Indian cinema.

The period is not a simple progression from failure to success, nor a straight line of technological advancement. It is a sequence of different creative and commercial approaches, each shaped by the film, the available platforms, audience behaviour and the wider entertainment environment.

For this investigation, the chronology is organised into five waves.

WAVE I · 2011

RA.ONE — The digital ambition

A film-led attempt to connect cinema with gaming, technology, merchandise, character development and an entertainment universe beyond the theatrical screen.

The question: Can a Bollywood film operate as a broader entertainment property?

WAVE II · 2013

CHENNAI EXPRESS — Integrated participation

An investigation of digital campaign mechanics, fan-club coordination, interactive experiences, brand associations and the integration of online engagement with film promotion.

The question: Can audience participation become an organised component of a film's marketing architecture?

WAVE III · 2014–2018

The changing landscape — Experimentation and transition

A period to be examined through the campaigns, platforms and creative decisions associated with films including ZERO, rather than characterised as a complete absence of digital innovation.

The question: How did campaign formats and the relationship between character, technology and audience evolve?

WAVE IV · 2023 ONWARDS

Pathaan, Jawan and Dunki — The renewed cinematic event

An examination of the public-facing campaigns, release strategies, official digital assets, audience amplification and the renewed scale of Shah Rukh Khan's film presence.

The question: What changed in the construction and communication of a major Shah Rukh Khan film release?

WAVE V · 2026

KING — The next frontier

The opportunity to examine whether fifteen years of evolving campaign practices can inform a new model of fan participation, intellectual property, digital storytelling and commercial collaboration.

The question: Can KING become a case study in the next evolution of Bollywood marketing?

2.1 The waves are a framework, not a verdict

The five-wave structure is an analytical framework for this thesis. It does not presume that one campaign was universally superior to another, that a particular film's commercial performance was caused by its marketing alone, or that the industry followed a single predetermined path.

Each wave must be assessed through its own documented campaign activity, creative mechanisms, audience response and commercial context.

The distinction is especially important when comparing campaigns separated by more than a decade. Social platforms, audience expectations, distribution systems and the economics of digital promotion changed substantially over this period. A meaningful comparison must account for those differences rather than treating raw engagement numbers as directly interchangeable.

The purpose of the chronology is therefore to identify what changed, what endured, and which earlier experiments might inform the future.

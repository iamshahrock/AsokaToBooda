ZERO (2018): BAUUA KI TOLI, ALEXA, SNAPCHAT AR AND CHARACTER-LED MARKETING

18.1 ZERO: the apparent lull meets its most important counterexample

ZERO is central to this investigation because it complicates the argument that Shah Rukh Khan's digital marketing innovation diminished after Chennai Express.

Released in 2018, the film presented Shah Rukh Khan as Bauua Singh, a character whose personality, physical scale, humour and distinctive worldview offered an unusual foundation for promotional storytelling.

The campaign explored character-led content, WhatsApp stickers, Bauua Ki Toli rewards, Alexa interaction and Snapchat augmented reality.

These were not simply conventional posters and trailers distributed through social media. They used the emerging capabilities of digital platforms to extend the character's presence into different audience environments.

ZERO demonstrates that the creative and technological ambition had not disappeared. The unresolved question is why those experiments did not visibly develop into a continuing, interconnected digital franchise on the scale contemplated by RA.ONE and Chennai Express.

18.2 Bauua Singh: the character as a promotional asset

Bauua Singh offered a distinctive promotional identity that could travel beyond the film's conventional publicity materials.

Character-led marketing can operate differently from star-led marketing.

Star-led promotion relies heavily on the recognition, personality and public presence of the actor. Character-led promotion creates an additional identity that can be used within the fictional world, in platform-native content, in digital interactions and in audience participation.

ZERO brought these two identities together: Shah Rukh Khan's established celebrity presence and the fictional personality of Bauua Singh.

This created an opportunity for audiences to engage with the character rather than simply receive information about the actor or the forthcoming film.

The significance is not that character-led marketing was invented by ZERO. It is that the campaign provided another example of the character becoming a vehicle for digital engagement.

18.3 Bauua Ki Toli: community, rewards and participation

Bauua Ki Toli extended the character's identity into a fan-participation framework.

The concept connected audience engagement with a recognisable character-led community and rewards. It gave fans a way to associate themselves with the campaign and participate in activities organised around the film.

This matters because it brings the analysis back to the relationship between a character, a fan community and the campaign's operating model.

There is, however, a distinction between a campaign-specific community and a persistent fan-franchising system.

A campaign can successfully organise participation and rewards around a single film without establishing the governance, technology, intellectual-property framework and continuing activity required to sustain the community between releases.

The available material supports examining Bauua Ki Toli as a character-led campaign initiative. It does not establish that it became a permanent, independently operating entertainment franchise.

18.4 Alexa: taking the character into a voice interface

The Alexa interaction is particularly relevant to the technology-led dimension of the thesis.

Voice interfaces offered a different way for audiences to encounter a film character. Rather than watching a video or reading a post, a user could interact through a voice-enabled device.

The campaign's use of Bauua in an Alexa-related experience illustrates how a character could be introduced into an existing technology environment.

This was a platform-specific form of character engagement, and its relevance lies in the interaction between the fictional identity and the capabilities of the platform.

The full technical assessment would require the original activation documentation: the available commands, user journey, duration, geographic availability, usage data and the precise responsibilities of the participating parties.

Without that material, the existence of the voice activation can be distinguished from any claim about its scale or commercial effectiveness.

18.5 Snapchat AR: bringing the character into the audience's camera

The Snapchat augmented-reality activity provides another important example.

Camera effects and augmented reality allow a film character or visual identity to become part of a user's own digital image or video.

This changes the relationship between promotional content and the audience.

A conventional poster presents a fixed image. An AR lens invites the user to activate an effect, create a personal visual asset and potentially share it with others.

In this sense, the technology offers a form of participation and personalisation.

It also demonstrates why ZERO cannot fairly be described as a campaign without technological innovation.

The more precise question is whether the character-led experiences, voice activation, camera effects and fan-reward mechanisms were integrated into a durable digital system—or remained separate promotional activities connected principally by the film's release.

18.6 Why ZERO does not invalidate the apparent-lull thesis

ZERO is not evidence of technological inactivity. It is evidence of continued experimentation.

The distinction between the early benchmark and ZERO is therefore not whether innovation occurred. It is the relationship between innovation and continuity.

WHAT ZERO DEMONSTRATES

Character-led digital promotion continued.

Fan participation and rewards remained part of the campaign landscape.

Voice interfaces and augmented reality offered new ways to experience a film character.

The promotional approach incorporated platform-specific technology.

WHAT REMAINS UNESTABLISHED

Whether the activations formed a unified, persistent digital platform.

Whether the audience relationships continued beyond the release window.

Whether the technology and campaign infrastructure were reused across subsequent releases.

Why the broader franchise-building ambitions associated with the earlier campaigns were not consistently visible.

The apparent lull should therefore be understood as a period of changing innovation, not the cessation of innovation.

ZERO is the final major case study in this part because it leaves the investigation with an unresolved tension: the technology and creative possibilities were still present, but the publicly documented record does not establish a continuing entertainment ecosystem that carried those possibilities forward.
